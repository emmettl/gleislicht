# Johannesburg — title to find

**An unnumbered Johannesburg motion study**

**Catalogue status:** feasibility and care investigation complete; motion study blocked pending data partnership and local review.

## Thesis under test

Johannesburg's mobility still bears the spatial violence of apartheid: settlement, employment, distance and transport cost remain unevenly arranged across the city-region. The work could make that inherited geography visible through present movement, but only if it does not reduce people to flows between a privileged centre and an undifferentiated periphery.

This candidate has two inseparable requirements: credible movement evidence for minibus taxi, bus and rail, and locally informed representational governance. Neither can be replaced by cinematic confidence.

## Feasibility verdict — 6 September 2026

| Scope | Status | Defensible interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rea Vaya scheduled proof | **Amber technically / amber-red for publication** | City tender and service documents contain routes, operating spans and timetable material, but this audit found no maintained public GTFS or equivalent open feed. Procurement PDFs are not a durable transit-data licence. |
| Gautrain schedule | **Amber** | Gautrain publishes current rider timetables and bus-route documents, but no versioned open machine feed or historical trajectory archive was established. |
| Minibus taxi movement | **Red** | No comprehensive authoritative open route, timetable, vehicle-position or volume source was found. This is the defining representational gap. |
| Spatial inequality context | **Green as aggregate research** | GCRO Quality of Life survey data and commuting research are documented, downloadable and licensed for reuse. They describe samples and aggregates, not vehicle paths. |
| Full public Motion Study | **Red** | Omitting minibus movement would visually reproduce a serious source bias; inventing it would be worse. Local co-design and a new data agreement are prerequisites. |

Johannesburg should remain paused. A Rea Vaya-only technical sketch may be useful for internal pipeline testing, but it must not be presented as the city's transport field or the proposed work.

## Official transport material found

The City of Johannesburg's 2026 [Rea Vaya operating tender specification](https://joburg.org.za/work_/Documents/2026-Tenders/Reavaya1B%28b%29-TOR-Final-19-March-2026-Signed-25-March-2026.pdf) contains route descriptions, distances, service information and timetables for Phase 1B; it describes operation from 05:00 to 22:00. The City's [Phase 1C service notice](https://joburg.org.za/media_/Documents/2025-Media-Statements/Rea-Vaya-Phase1ca-services-start-operating-on-1-december-2025.pdf) records additional service from December 2025.

Those documents prove that services and approved schedules exist. They are not a stable machine-readable publication, do not state a reusable data licence and may contain contractual rather than passenger-facing timing. A manual conversion would need City verification and written transformed-publication permission.

[Gautrain passenger information](https://www.gautrain.co.za/commuter/generalinformation) offers current frequencies, schedules and bus-route documents. The same limitations apply: version retention, machine identity and reuse terms are unresolved.

The audit found no official open source that adequately represents the minibus taxi system. Public route-planning apps and research datasets may contain partial coverage, but they must not be promoted to comprehensive authority without producer, date, collection method, consent and rights.

## Inequality and historical context

The [Gauteng City-Region Observatory data portal](https://www.gcro.ac.za/outputs/dataportal/) publishes spatial resources and Quality of Life survey data. GCRO's [QoL survey overview](https://www.gcro.ac.za/research/project/detail/overview-quality-life-survey/) describes seven iterations through 2023/24 and directs users to downloadable data and documentation under CC BY-SA 4.0. The [2023/24 commuting and transport brief](https://gcro.ac.za/outputs/data-briefs/detail/commuting-and-transport-qol7/) supplies current aggregate context.

These are probability-sample survey observations. Weighting, question wording, sample geography and uncertainty must remain attached. They can show reported trip purpose, mode, travel time, cost or access at permitted aggregate geography; they cannot place a traveller on a route or turn ward averages into continuous motion.

Apartheid-era boundaries and settlement policy require authoritative historical spatial sources and interpretation by historians/local scholars. Present inequality must not be attributed mechanically to a single historical layer, even where the relationship is strong.

## Care and governance gate

Before visual development beyond a private data sketch:

1. establish a paid local advisory group spanning transport research, taxi-sector knowledge, spatial history and affected communities;
2. agree terminology, geography, claims and what should not be visualised;
3. obtain an authoritative or co-produced bounded minibus route/volume dataset with consent and reuse terms;
4. publish uncertainty and source absence on the canvas, not only in notes; and
5. review whether a spectacular follow-camera trivialises long commutes or spatial exclusion.

Aggregate and survey sources should use the coarsest geography that supports the claim. No individual survey record, device trace or inferred home/work pair belongs in the client artifact.

## Evidence boundaries

| Intended claim | Evidence required | Present feasibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A Rea Vaya bus is scheduled on a route | Verified City timetable converted with permission | **Possible but not yet durable.** |
| A minibus follows a shown route | Authoritative/co-produced route evidence with collection date | **Blocked comprehensively.** |
| Residents experience unequal travel time/cost | Weighted GCRO variables at permitted aggregate geography | **Yes as survey statistics.** |
| A moving particle represents an actual commuter | Individual observed trajectory and consent | **Neither available nor required.** |
| Apartheid spatial policy causes a displayed present pattern | Historical evidence plus careful local interpretation | **Research question, not a renderer inference.** |

## Recommended next action

Do not build the signature scene. Convene the source partnership first. A possible future bounded study could connect Soweto, the CBD, Alexandra and Sandton using Rea Vaya, Gautrain and minibus aggregates, but its boundary should be chosen with local collaborators and only after all three layers have commensurable time, geography and rights.

### Source and care gate

- [ ] Obtain maintained machine-readable Rea Vaya data or City-verified conversion rights.
- [ ] Establish a lawful current Gautrain timetable/geometry archive.
- [ ] Co-produce or license bounded minibus route and aggregate-volume evidence.
- [ ] Retain GCRO survey licence, weights, geography and uncertainty.
- [ ] Form and compensate a local review group before the visual thesis is fixed.
- [ ] Define privacy, suppression, historical causality and on-canvas uncertainty rules.

**Exit:** Johannesburg advances only through a locally governed source partnership that makes currently underrepresented movement visible without turning uncertainty or inequality into spectacle.

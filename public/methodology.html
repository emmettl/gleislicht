Motion study 005 · provenance

Data in.
Light out.

Gleislicht is a cinematic reading of transport data—not a control room and not a live vehicle tracker. Rail, air and road each retain the very different certainty their source provides; the atmosphere is interpretive.

What the movement means

Position
Interpolated between consecutive scheduled stops. Unless explicitly marked otherwise, dots are not GPS positions.
Time
The clock is deterministic and scrubbable. The morning study covers two hours; large full-day studies load in three-hour chunks around the selected time. The compact Lake Lucerne–Rigi study loads its whole day on request.
Headway services
Where a source publishes an interval rather than exact departures, motion is illustrative and labelled as a headway model. Exact repeating departures retain timetable labels. Approximate arrivals use ≈; these marks do not identify tracked vehicles.
Lake Lucerne–Rigi
The two cogwheel approaches and the Weggis cableway follow mapped Federal Office of Transport alignments. Boat paths are shortest paths constrained to the simplified FOEN lake polygon, including shore boundaries and islands where mapped. They are not observed tracks, shipping lanes or navigation guidance. Nearby pier coordinates may be snapped to the mapped water boundary. The optional ascents from Vitznau and Arth-Goldau RB to Rigi Kulm add swissALTIRegio ground elevations sampled beneath the mapped railway, a reduced terrain mesh and lake context. Their profiles represent ground rather than surveyed track heights; tunnels are not inferred from surface elevations. Train size, route clearance and scenic playback are artistic. Authored lake-to-summit sequences use the shared timetable clock; cable sag remains future work.
Brightness
Route glow expresses scheduled frequency on a segment. Short trails show recent simulated movement, not historical location records.
Selection
Station selection reveals all scheduled paths touching that stop in the loaded study. Line and category selection dims unrelated movement.
Geometry
National trains use matched Federal Office of Transport infrastructure geometry. Zürich and Genève regional studies use published operator geometry where matched. Unresolved segments remain scheduled approximations, with major lake crossings corrected by the shoreline fallback.
Terrain
The Zürich–Chur corridor uses measured swissALTIRegio elevations and named SBB tunnel records. Kiental–Griesalp uses a separate swissALTI3D crop and road-following OpenStreetMap geometry. Terrain meshes, lake surfaces, route profiles and camera scale are prepared offline; lighting, vertical emphasis and vehicle forms are artistic.
Air
LUFTRAUM is a separately loaded historical observation layer. Aircraft positions, altitude and groundspeed come from ADS-B receiver data; heading and vertical rate are derived between samples, and gaps longer than 45 seconds are not interpolated. Altitude is visually compressed.
Road
AUTO is a separately loaded traffic-flow reconstruction. Its national morning study uses recorded ASTRA one-minute flow and speed observations from 8 September 2026 across 718 accepted directional counter sites and 609 sections. Warm-white and amber marks are synthetic samples of aggregate light- and heavy-vehicle density; no mark represents a tracked automobile. A representative A1 Zürich calibration remains only as a loading fallback.

Sources and packaging

  • Swiss GTFS Static supplies routes, service calendars, trips, stops and stop times.
  • Federal Office of Transport rail network supplies national infrastructure alignment.
  • Federal Office of Transport cableways supplies the mapped Weggis–Rigi Kaltbad alignment (installation 71.105).
  • swissBOUNDARIES3D supplies the simplified national outline.
  • swissALTIRegio supplies the measured terrain and route elevation profile for Zürich–Chur and both Rigi railway approaches.
  • SBB railway tunnels supplies named portals and published lengths for underground sections of the Zürich–Chur corridor.
  • swissALTI3D supplies the measured high-resolution terrain for Kiental–Griesalp; its road trace is derived from OpenStreetMap contributors under the ODbL.
  • Federal open hydrography is simplified offline into the major lake layer, with source attribution retained in the artifact.
  • ZVV / VBZ GTFS and TPG / SITG provide regional line geometry.
  • ADSB.lol historical data, distributed under ODbL 1.0, supplies the optional observed-aircraft study for 07:00–08:00 CEST on 4 September 2026.
  • ASTRA / FEDRO realtime road counters define the DATEX II flow and speed contract; the separately published Measurement Site Table supplies Road Study 001's detector locations.

The browser never receives the source GTFS archive. Repeatable Node scripts validate and compress each study into small static JSON artifacts. National 24-hour data is split so the initial morning view remains fast.

Gornergrat: a dated map and summit ascent

The optional Gornergrat study uses Swiss GTFS 20260902 for 4 September 2026, restricted to Gornergratbahn agency 121, route 93-48-j26-1 and cogwheel type 116. It retains 54 services: 26 each way between Zermatt GGB and Gornergrat, plus one each way between Zermatt GGB and Riffelalp. Seven source stops include Zermatt Ferienhaus legendär on the trains that actually call there. There are no frequency templates in this fixture.

The journey selector offers 26 public summit ascents and 26 complete descents, following their exact calls on the shared clock. The direction control selects ascent or descent and retains the optional terrain preference. Its default is train 237, Zermatt GGB 12:00 to Gornergrat 12:33 via Findelbach, Riffelalp, Riffelberg and Rotenboden. Selecting another departure pauses at its start; arrival pauses playback; replay and manual-selection exit preserve normal exploration. Runtime validation reconciles source date, feed, archive hash, route identity, calls, boarding flags and geometry.

All 281 segment occurrences use mapped FOT paths simplified at 10 m. Endpoint matching permits at most 100 m; the largest actual difference is 67.1 m at Zermatt GGB. Source stop coordinates remain unchanged. The default view is a 2D timetable illustration; optional measured outdoor terrain is described below. Movement is interpolated, without live tracking. Short workings are never extended to the summit. The 4.2 KiB compressed map loads on request and exposes retry and dated links.

The operator's timetable page, checked on 8 September 2026, supplies seasonal context and current travel guidance. This single source day does not establish seasonal comparisons, preceding-day spillover, future availability or guaranteed connections. Source archive, FOT hashes, reconciled calls and per-pair geometry checks are retained in the study audit.

Gornergrat measured outdoor terrain

All 26 dated summit ascents and 26 complete descents can follow the measured landscape on their existing clock. Terrain combines swissALTIRegio (28 May 2026) landscape heights and separate railway XYZ axes from swissTLM3D 2026-02, both in LV95 / LN02. The native 10 m landscape is simplified to a 193 × 222 grid at approximately 44 m spacing. Horizontal and vertical scales are equal; the train marker is enlarged and its motion follows three-dimensional rail distance between actual timetable calls.

Active narrow-gauge cogwheel axes provide heights on the mapped FOT plan alignment, densified at most every 15 m. All 26 paths produce the same 654 railway samples, including both six- and seven-call patterns. The maximum axis offset is 14.43 m; mapped endpoint elevations are 1,604.3 m at Zermatt and 3,087.5 m at Gornergrat. These are source values at mapped path endpoints, not precision claims or advertised station altitudes. Trains that omit Ferienhaus legendär do not gain an extra stop.

Missing heights or offsets over 60 m fail generation. Tunnels, galleries, other covered or unrecognised structures and offsets above 25 m remain on the map. Conservative masks extend 30 m around adjacent samples and merge gaps up to 60 m. Four ranges cover three tunnel areas and a longer gallery, totalling 18.87% of the mapped distance including margins. They do not claim surveyed portal locations. Underground rails are never raised onto the terrain.

The optional 78.1 KiB compressed artifact loads only when selected. Terrain/map transitions preserve the clock and playback state; actual station calls, departure changes, arrival pause and replay remain available. Failed requests or validation keep the map with explicit retry. The audit retains source/member/raster hashes, all ascent path checks, selected feature identities and source years, station height comparisons and masks. Every complete descent is independently checked against the source railway and matches the 654 uphill XYZ samples in reverse, with identical source feature and structure identities. Its own source timetable determines motion: the default train 234 leaves Gornergrat at 11:55 and reaches Zermatt GGB at 12:39. Only audited downhill trip IDs can reverse the terrain route and masks; an older ascent-only artifact retains map playback with retry. No additional service dates or passenger connections are established. Landscape and railway XYZ: © swisstopo; plan alignment: Federal Office of Transport.

Jungfrau: valley railways, cogwheel trains and Eiger Express

The optional JUNG study uses Swiss GTFS 20260902 for 4 September 2026: 130 Berner Oberland-Bahn services, 123 Wengernalpbahn services, 73 Jungfraubahn services and 1,237 Eiger Express timetable records. Operators and transport types come from exact source identities. The cableway records are published timetable entries, not tracked cabins or a physical cabin roster. This fixture has no frequency templates.

All 2,564 segment occurrences follow mapped 2D FOT alignments. Matten and Grindelwald Terminal are inserted on audited infrastructure segments within six metres of their timetable stops. Interlaken Ost platform 2 resolves to the BOB platform 1–2 infrastructure node; original timetable coordinates and identifiers are retained.

Eiger Express uses exact FOT installation 75.014. Its mapped endpoints differ from the shared timetable stop coordinates at Grindelwald Terminal and Eigergletscher by 213 m and 136 m. These differences remain disclosed; no walking connector or guaranteed interchange is inferred. The operator’s network guide establishes the intended approaches, and its Eiger Express page identifies the tricable system.

This first study is a map, with scheduled interpolation rather than live tracking. The Wengen ascent optionally adds measured outdoor terrain and separately sourced rail heights; tunnels and covered sections retain the map. Vertical cable behaviour is not modelled. The clock uses one dated service day; preceding-day spillover, seasonal closure comparisons and additional Jungfrau-region branches are outside this fixture.

Jungfrau approach guide and dated ascent

The guide shows three source-identified approaches with selectable stations. Its links require forward call pairs on the exact route, agency and transport mode. The first playable ascent joins three trains from Interlaken Ost through Lauterbrunnen and Kleine Scheidegg to Jungfraujoch. Seventeen dated compositions reconcile against 69 upward source trips, including public pickup and drop-off restrictions.

No internal Lauterbrunnen transfer rule is present in the retained GTFS. The composition explicitly uses an editorial ten-minute allowance there. At Kleine Scheidegg, the exact WAB-to-JB platform pair has a three-minute GTFS minimum. The composition adds ten minutes for the operator’s advance boarding guidance, checked on 8 September 2026. Waiting intervals are the resulting published arrival-to-departure gaps; the one-hour maximum is an editorial limit. Reservations, available seats and guaranteed connections are not established by this map.

The default composition leaves Interlaken Ost at 12:04, waits 34 minutes at Lauterbrunnen and 20 at Kleine Scheidegg, and reaches Jungfraujoch at 14:41 on 4 September 2026. The clock follows the selected train on each leg and holds station focus during waits, with no walking animation. Arrival pauses playback. Evidence requires the matching source date, feed, archive hash, calls and complete geometry; changed or missing evidence removes affected choices. Optional measured terrain follows outdoor railway sections on this same clock, with map fallback through tunnels and covered structures.

Jungfrau measured terrain: outdoors and underground

The optional Wengen ascent terrain combines swissALTIRegio (28 May 2026) landscape heights with railway XYZ axes from swissTLM3D 2026-02. Both use LV95 / LN02. The native 10 m landscape is simplified to a 193 × 251 grid at approximately 85 m spacing, with equal horizontal and vertical scale. The camera clears the sampled landscape; an enlarged train marker and route overlay aid legibility over the simplified mesh.

Rail height is interpolated from compatible active narrow-gauge source axes onto the mapped FOT plan alignment, sampled at most every 15 m. Cogwheel flags are required on both upper legs. Matches over 60 m fail generation; offsets over 25 m, tunnels, galleries, underpasses, buildings and unrecognised structure classes stay on the map. Visibility masks extend 30 m around adjacent samples and merge across gaps up to 60 m. These conservative display margins do not claim surveyed portal positions.

The Jungfraujoch rail endpoint is 3,453.5 m in the source, below the native ground sample of 3,476.6 m. Its mapped tunnel remains in 2D. Underground track is never raised to the surface or drawn as an outdoor terrain route. No tunnel interior or cable sag is invented. Movement is timetable interpolation along three-dimensional railway distance, with station dwells and both transfer waits retained. Transitions between map and terrain preserve the clock and playback state.

The 108.7 KiB compressed artifact loads only on request and must match the dated timetable, source identities, calls and geometry checks. Loading or validation failures retain the map with an explicit retry. The retained audit records source archive/member hashes, railway feature identities and years, native raster hash, masks, matching offsets and station heights. Landscape and railway source: © swisstopo; mapped plan alignment: Federal Office of Transport.

The Rigi connections guide

The optional schematic shows selected connections through eleven source stops: the two railway approaches via Vitznau and Arth-Goldau, their common Rigi Staffel and Rigi Kulm destinations, the Weggis cableway, and lake piers. Selecting a stop reveals its timetable services on the map while preserving the clock and playback state.

Stop identities come from the retained GTFS source IDs and platform variants. The Weggis pier and cableway valley station remain distinct, as do the railway and cableway stations at Kaltbad. Vitznau’s boat and railway use one shared timetable stop. Transport links require matching operator, route and service-call evidence; the two dashed interchange links require the audited source date and archive hash.

This is a schematic, not to scale. Intermediate stops are omitted. Lines represent connections rather than track layouts, and dashed links are not walking routes or guaranteed connections. The lake-to-summit sequences below retain their own transfer-time rules.

A day on lake and mountain

The Rigi day rhythm uses the same 4 September 2026 fixture: 89 boat journeys, 28 Vitznau railway journeys (including the early partial Staffelhöhe services), 22 Arth-Goldau railway journeys and 51 Weggis cableway journeys. Both directions are included. Bands merge scheduled journeys from origin departure to final arrival, including station stops. Gaps show quiet periods in this dated timetable; “underway” does not mean every vehicle is moving at that instant.

Next-departure controls jump to the next later origin departure and pause the shared clock. The display includes all modes independently of the map filter. One late boat arrives at 24:02: the band clips at the 24:00 study boundary without claiming the service has finished. Returning to the first departure goes backward within the same day. Other operating dates, seasonal closures and live availability are outside this fixture.

Lake to summit: the Vitznau interchange

The optional RIGI sequence uses the 4 September 2026 timetable from Swiss GTFS 20260902. Ten daytime boats from Luzern Bahnhofquai pair with the first eligible Vitznau–Rigi Kulm train. Each pair has six scheduled minutes to change. Gaps above 30 minutes are excluded from this authored composition; that limit is editorial.

The source transfers.txt specifies a 60-second minimum at the shared Vitznau timetable stop, ch:1:sloid:8464. The operator's Vitznau arrival guide describes a 50-metre walk between dock and valley station. A shared source location does not remove that walk. These are scheduled intervals, not guaranteed connections, accessibility assessments or live travel advice.

The map clock follows the boat only until its Vitznau arrival, holds at the interchange, then follows the uphill railway. No walking motion is invented. Boat paths are modelled within the cartographic lake boundary, not GPS tracks or validated shipping lanes. The railway uses mapped FOT infrastructure. Both sequence railway legs can now follow the selected service in measured terrain on the same timetable clock; independent scenic playback remains available.

Via Weggis: the alternate sequence adds the cableway and a change at Rigi Kaltbad. The feed requires 20 minutes uphill from either Weggis pier to the valley station (15 minutes in reverse), then five minutes from the cableway summit station to the railway. The operator’s Rigi Kulm arrival guide separately describes a 15-minute Weggis walk and 200 metres between the Kaltbad stations.

The default 12:12 boat arrives at Weggis at 12:53, followed by the 13:40–13:50 cableway and the 14:35–14:47 cogwheel segment from Kaltbad to Rigi Kulm. Both long intervals (47 and 45 minutes) stay on the clock. Ten daytime chains meet this alternative’s editorial 60-minute maximum per interchange. This is a slower composition, not a guaranteed or recommended connection. The railway is followed only after boarding at Kaltbad. The cableway uses its mapped 2D alignment; no pedestrian geometry, cable sag or observed cabin positions are invented.

Timetable terrain: during a railway leg, “Follow this train in terrain” preserves the selected service, clock, pause state and playback rate. Matched station fractions use that train’s arrival and departure calls, holding through station dwell times. The Weggis approach enters at Kaltbad; it does not replay the earlier Vitznau section. Arrival at Rigi Kulm pauses playback. Returning to the map keeps the time, and scrubbing into an earlier mode restores its map stage. The optional terrain is ground beneath mapped rail, with a stylised vehicle and smoothed curve, not surveyed track or tunnel heights.

Limits, privacy and accessibility

Realtime: rail remains a scheduled simulation. Swiss GTFS Realtime offers predictions and alerts rather than national GPS vehicle positions; it will only be presented as realtime-adjusted interpolation once a paired, timestamped feed is operating. LUFTRAUM is explicitly historical observed data, not a live aircraft display. AUTO uses a validated historical series recorded by Gleislicht from ASTRA's latest-minute feed; it is not a live traffic display.

Privacy: Gleislicht contains no analytics, advertising, accounts or personal tracking. Language preference is stored locally in the browser. Add ?perf=1 to the URL for an on-device frame-rate monitor; its measurements are never transmitted.

Accessibility: keyboard search, reduced interface density on small screens, labelled controls and a non-WebGL explanation are built in. The map remains a visual artwork; the status cards and search results carry the essential textual information.

Export: supporting browsers can record a silent twelve-second canvas loop locally. The video is assembled and downloaded on-device; no frames are uploaded.

Visual design is interpretive. Schedules, times and source metadata are factual; terrain ambience, glow, trails and music are artistic encodings.
